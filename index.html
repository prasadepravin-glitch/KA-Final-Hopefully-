Six-region framework (Bombay/Kittur Karnataka, Kalyana Karnataka, Central Karnataka, Old Mysuru, Coastal Karnataka, Bengaluru) is an analytical lens linking society, geography, economy and electoral behaviour — per the report, not an official administrative division.

  Physical & administrative profile

  • Area 1.91 lakh sq. km · 31 districts · 320 km coastline.
  • Three physical systems: Western Ghats & Malnad, the inland Maidan plateau, coastal plains.
  • Western Ghats — a UNESCO World Heritage property — form the water divide feeding east- and west-flowing rivers.
  • East-flowing: Cauvery (804 km, 42.2% of basin in Karnataka, 320 km in-state) & Krishna (43.74% of basin, ~60% of Karnataka drains to it). West-flowing: Sharavati, Kali, Netravathi et al. — catchment 26,214 sq. km.
  • Elevation: Northern plateau 300–600 m · Central 450–700 m · Southern 600–900 m; Mullayyanagiri (1,925 m) is the highest peak.
  • Forests: 43,382 sq. km recorded forest area; 5 Tiger Reserves, 5 National Parks, 30 Wildlife Sanctuaries.
  • Climate: coastal tropical monsoon; south interior savanna; north interior semi-arid steppe. Hottest month May; coldest January.

  Institutions & the asymmetric state

  • Bicameral legislature: 224-member Legislative Assembly + 75-member Legislative Council.
  • Main seat — Vidhana Soudha, Bengaluru (first session of re-organised state, 1956); Suvarna Vidhana Soudha, Belagavi hosts the winter session.
  • Article 371J (98th Amendment, 2012): special provisions for the seven-district Kalyana Karnataka region, with its own Development Board (KKRDB).
  • Rural: Gram / Taluk / Zilla Panchayats under the Karnataka Gram Swaraj & Panchayat Raj system. Urban: Municipal Corporations & Municipalities.
  • High Court principal bench Bengaluru, with permanent benches at Dharwad and Kalaburagi.
  • Governor is constitutional head; executive power rests with the CM and Council of Ministers.

  Health & education — NFHS-6 (2023–24)

  • Access is strong: 99.7% households with electricity · 97.3% improved drinking water · 96.3% improved sanitation · 98.6% bank/post-office account.
  • Maternal & child health: 82.4% ANC in first trimester · 98.7% institutional births · 90.2% full immunisation (12–23 m). MMR ≈ 63 / lakh; IMR ≈ 19 / 1,000*.
  • Nutrition is the problem area: report cites 48.3% stunting, 19.7% wasting, 27.8% underweight among under-fives†; only 19.7% of children 6–23 months get an adequate diet.
  • NCD burden rising: 41.2% of women / 36.4% of men (15–49) overweight or obese; hypertension in ~25%+ of adults.
  • Education: GER — secondary 101.3%, higher secondary 61.4%, higher education 36.2%. Literacy ≈ 75.4%; Dakshina Kannada >88% vs Yadgir–Kalaburagi 57–68%.
  • Digital gender gap: 57.9% of women vs 84.3% of men used the internet recently.

†As cited in the report; the accompanying deck cites 26.5% stunting for NFHS-6 — figures differ by source table. *Notes document.

  Economy anchors & infrastructure

  • Sectoral split: services ~70% · agriculture ~19% · industry ~11% of GSDP.
  • New Mangalore Port — Karnataka's only Major Port — handled 46.01 million tonnes in FY 2024–25 (petroleum, fertiliser, coal, iron ore).
  • Pavagada Solar Park: 2,000 MW planned capacity — flagship renewable infrastructure.
  • Industrial Policy 2025–30 pivots to aerospace & defence, electronics, manufacturing and logistics.
  • Tourism: Hampi & the Hoysala ensembles (UNESCO), Western Ghats biodiversity, coastal & pilgrimage circuits — success measured by local jobs and conservation, not footfall alone.
  • Agriculture is politically decisive: rainfall, crop prices and farm incomes move the vote in the dry belts; water policy spans multiple departments and two inter-state river systems.
History & Lineage

From Halmidi to Karnataka: 1,500 years in one line

Indigenous dynasties (Kadambas → Chalukyas → Rashtrakutas → Hoysalas → Vijayanagara → Wodeyars), a sultanate interlude and Mysuru's Hyder–Tipu, then the merger of five administrative fragments into one Kannada state in 1956 — renamed Karnataka in 1973.

Built Heritage

Monuments that carry the political memory

Reading the arc. The report's core claim: Karnataka's present political economy is an inheritance — dynasty, unification and caste coalitions still set today's battlegrounds. The 2023 mandate was a bet on welfare; its fiscal bill is now the central question.

Caste, Society & Reservation

The survey that changed the conversation

Karnataka's OBC framework evolved over a century of committees and judgments. The 2015 Socio-Economic & Educational Survey — submitted in 2024 — found backward classes ≈ 70% of the state and recommended raising OBC quota from 32% to ~51%: politically explosive, and a defining 2028 issue.

Survey findings — community shares (reported ranges)

Socio-Economic & Educational Survey (2015, submitted Feb 2024)

Ranges as reported: SC ≈19.5% · Muslims ≈14–16% · Vokkaligas ≈10–14% · Lingayats ≈11% · Kurubas ≈7–8%. Backward Classes overall ≈ 70%.

*The ~75% figure is an illustrative arithmetic: 51% OBC + 15% SC + 3% ST = 69%; adding pending SC/ST revision demands (17% + 7%) would take Karnataka to ~75% — far beyond the 50% Indra Sawhney ceiling. Labelled illustrative; not an enacted proposal.

● Lingayat bloc

Veerashaiva-Lingayats ≈11% (survey). Core of Kittur & Central Karnataka politics.

  • Anchor of BJP consolidation since the Yediyurappa era; Panchamasali sub-sect demands 2A OBC status.
  • Rajiv Gandhi's 1990 dismissal of CM Veerendra Patil catalysed the realignment away from Congress.

● Vokkaliga bloc

≈10–14% (survey). Old Mysuru heartland; Gangadikara sub-sect dominant.

  • Base of JD(S) (Deve Gowda family) vs D.K. Shivakumar's Congress drive — the 2023 split handed Congress the south.
  • Category 3A (4%) reservation, raised to 6% in the March 2023 reclassification.

● AHINDA

Alpasankhyataru (minorities) + Hindulidavaru (backward classes) + Dalitaru (Dalits).

  • Siddaramaiah's signature coalition; delivered the 2013 and 2023 Congress majorities.
  • Survey shows SCs ≈19.5% — the largest single bloc; internal SC sub-classification now in play.

● Minorities

Muslims ≈14–16% (survey) — among the state's largest communities.

  • Category 2B (4% quota) scrapped in March 2023, redirected to EWS; Supreme Court interim order restored status quo.
  • Strongest in Kalyana Karnataka and Bengaluru's peri-urban belt.

Fiscal Health & Guarantees

A revenue-deficit state, still inside the KFRA cage

Karnataka adheres to the Karnataka Fiscal Responsibility Act — fiscal deficit inside 3% of GSDP, liabilities inside 25% — but the five guarantees have shifted a historically revenue-surplus state into a structural revenue deficit. Budget 2026–27: outlay ₹4,48,004 crore (+13%).

Measured social impact. A Karnataka study of ≈26,000 women found female labour-force participation rose from 25.3% → 30.2% in Shakti's first year; 57% reported better job access, 49% more community participation. Reported gains: mobility, liquidity (Gruha Lakshmi), food security (Anna Bhagya), household savings (Gruha Jyothi).
CAG's critique. Guarantees ≈ 20% of revenue receipts and 27% of own revenue (2024–25); borrowing finances them; universal design vs targeting; funding cuts hit nutrition, local bodies and rural development; debt service crowds out capital expenditure.

  Fiscal stress points (Budget 2026–27)

  • Unreleased central funds: 15th Finance Commission special grant ₹5,495 cr + state-specific ₹6,000 cr not released.
  • Jal Jeevan Mission: of the ₹46,102 cr central share sanctioned, ₹19,102 cr pending; state has itself released ₹27,098 cr + another ₹15,500 cr in 2025–26.
  • Reduced central shares in Centrally Sponsored Schemes and Central Sector allocation issues add pressure.
  • Committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, interest): ₹1,85,025 cr; interest payments approaching ₹48,000 cr+.
  • Own-tax revenue is the anchor: ₹1,93,100 cr (RE 2025–26) — ~70% of revenue receipts; GoI transfers ₹66,802 cr.

  Delivery friction

  • DBT bottlenecks: Gruha Lakshmi payments delayed 1–2 months by NPCI/Aadhaar-seeding failures.
  • BPL purge backlash: automated IT/GST data-matching wrongly revoked valid cards; drive paused.
  • Grain supply: central FCI restrictions forced ₹170/month cash-in-lieu under Anna Bhagya, adding audit & leakage risk.
  • Transport & power utilities: KSRTC/BMTC/NWKRTC/KKRTC and ESCOMs wait on quarterly subsidy reimbursements — fleet ageing, tariff pressure on industry.
Bengaluru

The engine of Karnataka — and its governance test

Bengaluru Urban alone holds 28 of 224 Assembly seats and anchors roughly 40% of state GSDP* and over a third of India's IT exports. Yet the metropolis has had no full municipal election since 2015 — a governance crisis the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act is meant to resolve.

Bengaluru in the state economy*

Illustrative: ~40% of GSDP per the notes

Bengaluru is the primary driver of own-source revenue and GST collections — the fiscal surplus of the city funds welfare across the state.

  The new governance architecture

  • Greater Bengaluru Governance Act: the single BBMP is split into five city corporations — Central, North, East, West, South — to decentralise ward delivery.
  • Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA): CM-chaired apex body supervising metropolitan planning, transit and utilities across parastatals (BDA, BWSSB, BMTC, BMLTA).
  • The 74th Amendment question: a CM-chaired authority coordinating appointed administrators tests the promise of empowered, elected local self-government — accountability without an elected council is the central tension.
  • Brand Bengaluru framework: Namma Metro & suburban rail expansion, lake rejuvenation, municipal tax restructuring.

  Water

The city's lakes (Bellandur, Varthur, Hebbal) were engineered as a rainwater cascade system; urbanisation has turned them into flood-and-sewage flashpoints.

  • Watershed ridge splits Arkavathi (Cauvery basin) & South Pennar flows.
  • Water security is now a top civic-election issue; Cauvery allocation links the city to inter-state politics.

  Traffic & floods

Gridlock and seasonal flooding are the city's daily political currency — potholes, garbage and drainage dominate election discourse.

  • Overlapping mandates (GBA/BBMP, BDA, BWSSB, BMLTA) create execution friction on stormwater drains.
  • Metro + suburban rail are the flagship mobility answers.

  Civic finance & land

Without an elected council, ward-level accountability and budget scrutiny are weakened.

  • B-Khata → A-Khata regularisation backlog is a persistent administrative bottleneck.
  • Municipal tax restructuring is promised under Brand Bengaluru.
  • Lower urban turnout, class-and-service politics vs caste in the metro.
Why 2028 hinges on this. The report lists Bengaluru's governance reforms among the five decisive factors for 2028: “whether the governance reforms in Bengaluru result in noticeable improvements to services.” The 28 urban seats are the state's biggest swing block — BJP holds urban middle-class belts; Congress the peri-urban, minority and lower-income wards.
Elections & 2028 Outlook

A state that splits its vote

Karnataka's electorate systematically separates state and national choices: in 2023 it gave Congress 135/224 seats on a welfare promise; one year later it gave the NDA 19/28 Lok Sabha seats. Heading into 2028, the fight is over entitlements, caste data and the region-by-region swing vote.

The Eve of 2028

New leadership, new alliance, same questions

  Congress in transition

  • D.K. Shivakumar is CM from June 2026 — a Vokkaliga face over an AHINDA coalition; Siddaramaiah plans retirement from electoral politics.
  • Dr. G. Parameshwara is Deputy CM — senior Dalit leader, ex-KPCC president (2010–18), PhD (Plant Physiology).
  • Yathindra S. (Siddaramaiah's son) in the Cabinet — succession politics inside the AHINDA bloc.
  • Test: break Karnataka's 40-year pattern of voting out incumbents.

  The NDA challenge

  • BJP–JD(S) alliance: BJP's organisation + JD(S)'s Vokkaliga base — but vote transfer and seat-sharing friction persist (2024 cross-voting signs).
  • Anchors: Lingayat mobilisation in Kittur & Central Karnataka; coastal Hindutva networks.
  • Target: the Deve Gowda family's Old Mysuru citadel vs DKS's consolidation.

Disputes & Unresolved Questions

The open files of the Karnataka state project

Unification solved the language question; it did not dissolve the borders, rivers and regional gaps it inherited. These are the disputes the report leaves open.

  Cauvery water-sharing

  • 804 km river; 42.2% of the basin in Karnataka, 320 km in-state; rises at Talakaveri, Kodagu.
  • Mandya's sugarcane–paddy economy lives on KRS/Kabini releases — canal schedules are election issues.
  • Oversight: Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA) allocations shape seasonal farm planning.
  • Inter-state politics with Tamil Nadu remains a permanent feature of southern Karnataka politics.

  Belagavi border (Maharashtra)

  • Maharashtra claims Belagavi, Nippani and 814 border villages; dispute running since 1957.
  • Local arena: Maharashtra Ekikaran Samiti (MES) vs pro-Kannada outfits (Karnataka Rakshana Vedike); leaders Shubham Shelke & Narayana/Shivarame Gowda.
  • State's answer: Suvarna Vidhana Soudha at Belagavi — a permanent legislature to assert sovereignty (largely underused outside winter sessions).
  • Naming counter-move: "Mumbai-Karnataka" renamed Kittur Karnataka (Nov 2021).

  Mahadayi (Kalasa–Banduri)

  • Karnataka wants to divert Mahadayi tributaries into the Malaprabha basin for drinking water in dry northern districts.
  • Goa opposes on ecological and drinking-water grounds; Maharashtra is the third party.
  • Status: contested inter-state water dispute — a live grievance in Kittur Karnataka.

  Article 371J & Kalyana Karnataka

  • 98th Amendment (2012): reservations in education & government jobs for locals of the seven-district region; KKRDB administers development action plans.
  • CAG's finding: the region still underperforms on socio-economic parameters — an outcome problem, not a funding problem.
  • Open question: does special status convert into better education, health, infrastructure and employment?

  Kasaragod & sub-national identities

  • Kasaragod: linguistic/cultural border contentions with Kerala — place names, minority-language rights, periodic access disputes.
  • Tulu Eighth Schedule demand: an active campaign from Coastal Karnataka for constitutional recognition.
  • Kodava identity remains a distinct strand in Kodagu politics.

  Western Ghats vs livelihoods

  • Kasturirangan & Gadgil committee implementation battles conservation against agrarian rights in Malnad.
  • Upper Bhadra & Upper Krishna Stage III irrigation projects are capital-constrained by the guarantee bill.
  • The deeper question from the report: spreading growth beyond Bengaluru while keeping fiscal space and institutional capacity.
The most important analytical conclusion: “The next development challenge facing Karnataka is not merely achieving higher growth but rather spreading that growth beyond Bengaluru while at the same time maintaining fiscal space and institutional capacity.” — Karnataka State Overview report, Executive Summary.
